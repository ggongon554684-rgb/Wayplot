WayPlot CRM
A Multi-Tenant, Map-Based Customer Relationship Management System for Land and Real Estate Businesses
Gabriel Ellis G. Gongon | Revised 10-30-26
Rationale
Buying and selling land depends on relationships and timely follow-up. Yet many land and real estate businesses, from small brokerages selling lots on behalf of owners to developers selling their own, still keep their customers in group chats, phone contacts, and spreadsheets. This causes real problems. An inquiry gets buried in a busy chat and the lead goes cold. When an agent leaves or changes phone numbers, the customer contacts they built go with them. Nobody keeps a clear history of who asked about which lot, what was said, and what happened next, so the business cannot tell which lots attract the most interest or which customers have gone quiet. Details about each lot and its owner sit in separate places from the customers who ask about it, which makes records incomplete and hard to compare.
Customer Relationship Management (CRM) software exists to solve this kind of problem by keeping all customer information and interactions in one organized place. However, popular CRM products are built for general sales teams. They do not understand land. A parcel, its boundary, its photos, its owner, and the customers asking about it are not connected, and the cost and complexity of enterprise plans put them out of reach for small land businesses.
WayPlot CRM is designed to fill this gap. It is an internal customer management system that a land or real estate business uses to run its own sales. Customers and landowners are records inside the system. They are not users and they do not log in. Only the business’s staff do. WayPlot is offered as a subscription: each business (a tenant) receives its own private workspace, and a Master (Super Admin) manages tenants, plans, and subscriptions across the platform. There are three tiers, so a small business can start with the basics and grow into advanced features. Each higher tier includes everything in the tier below it.

What sets WayPlot apart from a generic CRM is that every record is tied to a place. Agents draw each parcel as a polygon or marker on an embedded map, label it, add a description and photos, and record the landowner’s details on it. Each lead then links to the parcel it is about, so the business sees its land inventory and its customer interest in one view. The map is built on open tools (for example Leaflet.js with OpenStreetMap), which keeps development and running costs low.
By keeping customers, parcels, and conversations in one organized system, WayPlot CRM helps small land businesses respond on time, keep their customer relationships when staff change, and decide using real data rather than scattered messages.

How the modules work together. Take one customer. Data Collection records her and the lot she asked about. Main transaction carries her from the first call to a confirmed Closed deal. Scope and Assumptions
• WayPlot is a desktop application for staff. It connects to a central database, so branches and the platform-wide reports share the same data.
• Customers (buyers) and landowners (sellers) are records, not users. There is no public map page, buyer login, or marketplace. A buyer-facing mobile app is future work.
• Customers are contacted outside the system by SMS or email, sent from the app, and by phone calls that staff log. A customer is messaged only if the record shows consent.
• The tenant may be a brokerage selling for landowners or a developer selling its own lots. The system works the same way: the owner recorded on the parcel is either the landowner or the company itself.
• The app keeps working when the internet drops, as described under Offline Behavior.
• WayPlot records payments, balances, and commissions, but it does not collect money. There is no online payment, and it is not a full accounting system. Customers never see their balance or pay through WayPlot.
• Money records follow the same rule as other records: staff see only what their role allows (see Who Can See What). WayPlot platform staff do not see a tenant’s customer or money records.
Users and Their Purpose
WayPlot has four roles. Every user has one role, and the role decides what they can do and what the CRM keeps track of for them. They depend on each other: agents work the leads, the Tenant Admin oversees the business, the Branch Manager oversees a branch, and the Super Admin keeps the platform and its subscriptions running.
RoleWho they arePurpose in the systemWhat the CRM does for themAgent / StaffA broker, agent, or salesperson working for a tenantPlot lots, capture customers, and work each lead to a saleKeeps contacts and communication history, shows each lead’s stage and score, sends reminders, and shows their own commissions and customers’ balancesTenant AdminThe owner or manager of a subscribing businessRun the account and oversee all agents, parcels, and leadsShows the whole pipeline, dashboards, and reports, and lets the admin assign leads and approve deals, payments, commissions, and promosBranch Manager (Tier C)The head of one branch of a tenantManage one branch’s agents, parcels, and leadsShows the branch pipeline, branch reports, and the branch’s commissionsSuper Admin (Master)The WayPlot platform staffRun the platform and its subscriptionsManages tenants and plans, and shows platform-wide analytics
People who are not users. Customers and landowners appear in the system only as records. A customer is a lead with contact details, a pipeline stage, and a history of every interaction. Once a deal closes, the customer becomes a Client and also has a balance and a payment history. A landowner is recorded on the parcel as owner name, contact details, and listing agreement. Agents create and update these records. The people themselves never sign in.

Lead Scoring
Every new lead starts cold. From Tier B, WayPlot adds points as staff log customer activity, and the total score sets the lead’s temperature. Scoring is separate from the pipeline stage: the stage says where the deal stands, and the score says how interested the customer appears. The values below are starting defaults that the Tenant Admin can adjust.

Everything about money starts at one moment: the Tenant Admin confirms a Closed deal. From then on the system keeps a deal record and everything attached to it. This is the Deals & Payments module. It is in Tier A because it is core to selling land. Tier B adds reports and payment reminders, and Tier C adds branch commission splits.
The deal record
When the Tenant Admin confirms Closed, the system creates one deal record that links the customer, the parcel, and the agent. A customer can have more than one deal, for example two lots, and each deal has its own balance.
PartWhat it holdsTermsPrice, discount, reservation fee, down payment, and whether the customer pays in full or by installment.Payment planInstallment amount, due dates, number of payments, and any late penalty.PaymentsEach payment received: date, amount, method, receipt number, and who recorded it.BalancePrice minus confirmed payments, the next due date, and Current or Overdue. The system works this out.Commissions and bonusesThe amount owed to each person and whether it is Pending, Approved, or Paid.Landowner shareFor a brokerage: the owner’s share and when it was paid out.DocumentsContract, deed of sale, title or tax declaration, buyer ID, and receipts.
Customer balance and payments
The contact page shows a short summary for each deal the customer has: the parcel, the balance, the next due date, and Current or Overdue. Opening a deal shows the full schedule and payment history. The balance is kept on the deal, not on the contact, so a customer with two lots has two balances.
The agent records a payment when money is received. The Tenant Admin confirms it, and only then does the balance go down. In Tier B, the system reminds the agent before each due date and when a payment is overdue, and texts the customer if consent is on record.
Commissions
A commission is created when the Tenant Admin confirms Closed. The tenant sets the rules once: a percentage or a fixed amount, split between the selling agent, the Branch Manager (Tier C), and the company. Rules can differ by parcel or by agent. Each commission has a status:
• Pending: created by the system when the deal is confirmed.
• Approved: the Tenant Admin agrees the amount.
• Paid: the Tenant Admin records the payout and the date.
Referral bonuses
When an agent captures a lead whose source is Referral, the agent records who referred it: an existing customer, a staff member, or an outsider with a name and contact number. The bonus is not created at capture. It is created when the referred customer’s deal is confirmed Closed, using the tenant’s referral rule, and it goes through the same Pending, Approved, and Paid steps as a commission.
A customer whose deal is confirmed Closed gets the status Client on their contact page. If that Client has referred other people, the page also shows the bonuses earned and their status.

Cancelled or defaulted deals
If a customer cancels or stops paying, the Tenant Admin marks the deal Cancelled or Defaulted and records what happens to the money already paid (refunded, partly refunded, or kept). The parcel returns to Available and unpaid commissions are cancelled. Commissions already paid are flagged for the Tenant Admin to decide.

Use Case Description: Capture and Work a Lead
This is the core use case of the system. It shows how the customer record, the parcel, the communication log, and the pipeline work together.
ItemDescriptionUse caseCapture and work a leadPrimary actorAgent / StaffSecondary actorTenant AdminTriggerA customer contacts the business by phone, walk-in, signboard, referral, or social mediaPreconditionsThe tenant has an active subscription. The agent is logged in. The parcel of interest has been plotted.PostconditionThe lead is recorded with its full history, and its stage reflects the latest outcome
Main flow
1. The customer contacts the business. The customer is outside the system.
2. The agent opens Capture lead and enters the customer’s name, contact details, source, parcel of interest, consent to be messaged, and who referred them if the source is a referral.
3. The system checks for an existing contact with the same phone number or email.
4. The system creates the lead at stage New and assigns it to the agent, or to another agent by the tenant’s distribution rule. In Tier B, the lead is labeled Cold with a score of 10.
5. The agent sends an SMS or email from the app, or calls, and the system logs the interaction against the lead and parcel. The stage moves to Contacted.
6. The agent schedules a viewing. The stage moves to Viewing, and a reminder is created.
7. After the viewing, the agent logs the outcome and moves the lead to Negotiation. In Tier B, the score is updated.
8. When the customer agrees to buy, the agent requests Closed. The Tenant Admin reviews and confirms, and the parcel status is updated to sold or leased. The system then creates the deal record and the commissions (see Close a Deal and Collect Payments).
9. In Tier B and above, the tenant’s dashboards update with the new result.

Alternative flows
• Existing contact: if the phone number or email matches, the system adds the new inquiry to that contact’s history instead of creating a duplicate.
• No response (Tier B and above): if the lead stays untouched past the reminder period, the system reminds the agent, and notifies the Tenant Admin if it stays unanswered.
• Agent leaves: the Tenant Admin reassigns the agent’s leads and parcels to another agent, and their history moves with them.
• Lead drops out: at any stage, the agent can mark the lead Lost and record the reason.
• No internet: the agent keeps working, and changes are saved as pending sync (see Offline Behavior).
• Inactive subscription: the tenant’s workspace becomes read-only until the subscription is reactivated.

Use Case Description: Close a Deal and Collect Payments
This use case starts where Capture and Work a Lead ends. It turns a confirmed sale into a deal record, tracks the customer’s balance, and works out who earns what.
ItemDescriptionUse caseClose a deal and collect paymentsPrimary actorAgent / StaffSecondary actorTenant AdminTriggerA customer agrees to buy or lease a parcelPreconditionsThe lead is at Negotiation. The tenant has an active subscription. The tenant has set its commission and referral rules.PostconditionThe deal is recorded, the parcel is marked sold or leased, the balance is tracked, and each commission and referral bonus exists and is visible to the person who earned it.
Main flow
1. The agent requests Closed and enters the agreed terms: price, discount, reservation fee, down payment, and full payment or installment plan.
2. The Tenant Admin reviews the terms and confirms Closed.
3. The system creates the deal record, updates the parcel status to sold or leased, and sets the customer’s status to Client.
4. The system creates the commissions for the agent, the Branch Manager (Tier C), and the company using the tenant’s rules. If the lead has a referrer, it also creates the referral bonus. All start as Pending.
5. When the customer pays, the agent records the payment with its amount, date, method, and receipt number.
6. The Tenant Admin confirms the payment. The system lowers the balance and sets the next due date.
7. In Tier B and above, the system reminds the agent before each due date and when a payment is overdue, and texts the customer if consent is on record.
8. The Tenant Admin approves the commissions and referral bonuses and later marks each one Paid with the date. Each person can see their own amount and status.
9. When the balance reaches zero, the system marks the deal Fully paid.

Alternative flows
• Payment not confirmed: the Tenant Admin returns it to the agent with a note, and the balance stays the same.
• Customer misses payments: the balance shows Overdue and reminders continue. The Tenant Admin can mark the deal Defaulted.
• Deal cancelled or defaulted: the Tenant Admin records what happens to the money already paid. The parcel returns to Available, unpaid commissions are cancelled, and commissions already paid are flagged for the Tenant Admin to decide.
• Referrer is not in the system: the agent enters the referrer’s name and contact number on the lead, and an outside referrer becomes a contact record.
• No internet: a payment recorded offline is saved as pending sync. Confirming payments and approving or paying commissions need a connection.
• Inactive subscription: the workspace is read-only, so no payments can be recorded.

Offline Behavior
Agents often work on site, where signal is poor. WayPlot keeps a local copy of recent data on each computer. Reading works offline, the main daily actions are saved as pending sync and upload when the connection returns, and everything else needs a connection.
Parcel photos are added, removed, and viewed only while connected; they are not cached or queued for offline sync.

Sync conflicts
• Every change records the time and the user who made it.
• For ordinary fields, the latest change wins, and the history keeps the earlier value.
• For important changes such as Closed, Sold, reassignment, payments, or commissions, the system does not overwrite silently. It flags the conflict for the Tenant Admin to review.
